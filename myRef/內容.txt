臺灣已進入超高齡社會，沿街收運的真正痛點並非「垃圾車在哪裡」，而是資訊未回答「以我的位置與腳程，現在出門是否仍從容」。出發決策面對到點預測、步行時間與資料新鮮度三層不確定性，故本研究不追求「讓ETA更準」，而是定義系統何時足以提出出發建議、何時必須降級。研究以臺中為場域，構築垃圾清運領域專屬之Domain AI Agent：將領域知識（清運時刻表、路網可達性、收運作業特性）封裝為白名單領域工具，LLM僅負責意圖辨識與說明，數值計算與決策由受控工具以JSON Schema驗證執行，將工具幻覺限制於可驗證邊界。系統以時間餘裕slack＝ETA−步行時間−安全緩衝建立出發判斷，緩衝隨資料延遲與預測誤差動態調整，後端以FastAPI結合PostGIS估算步行時間。評估採滾動式時間序列回測，比較歷史平均、距離速度與迴歸模型，以MAE、RMSE衡量預測效能，以誤判率為首要安全指標，並同時報告有效建議覆蓋率與降級成功率，輔以至少三十項異常情境測試。預期成果為ETA相對歷史平均基線達統計顯著改善、誤判率在覆蓋率不崩潰下受控、資料來源與降級原因完全揭露，釐清低頻更新資料下個人化出發建議的適用邊界。

When to Advise and When to Defer: A Domain-Specific Tool-Grounded Agent for Departure Decisions in Curbside Waste Collection

Abstract—As Taiwan enters a super-aged society, the real burden of curbside waste collection is not locating the garbage truck but deciding whether a resident, given their location and walking pace, can still leave home and arrive comfortably in time. This departure decision faces three compounding uncertainties: arrival-time prediction, walking time, and data freshness. Rather than refining arrival-time accuracy, this study defines when a system is entitled to recommend departure and when it must degrade. Using Taichung City as the study area, we build a domain-specific artificial intelligence agent that encapsulates waste-collection knowledge, including collection schedules, pedestrian network accessibility, and operational characteristics, as whitelisted tools. A large language model handles only intent recognition and explanation, whereas numerical computation and decisions are executed by controlled tools with schema-validated inputs and outputs, confining tool hallucination within verifiable boundaries. Feasibility is judged by a time margin, namely the predicted arrival time minus the walking time and a safety buffer, with the buffer adapted to data latency and prediction error; walking time is estimated with a spatial database. Rolling-origin backtesting compares historical-average, distance–speed, and regression predictors by mean absolute error and root-mean-square error, treats the false-feasibility rate as the primary safety metric, reports recommendation coverage and fallback success rate, and includes at least thirty anomaly scenarios. Expected outcomes include statistically significant improvement over the historical-average baseline, a bounded false-feasibility rate without collapse in coverage, full disclosure of data sources and fallback reasons, and an explicit applicability boundary for personalized departure advice under low-frequency data.

Index Terms—Arrival time prediction, decision support systems, domain-specific agents, graceful degradation, large language models, tool hallucination, waste collection.
